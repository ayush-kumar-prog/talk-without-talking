The story

Why I'm building this.

I talk to my computer all day. But most places I work, I can't say a word out loud.

An open office. A train. A library. A room with someone asleep in it. Voice is the fastest way to tell a computer what you want, and it only works when nobody else is around.

So I built something that reads the words I murmur, and types them. No microphone. Nothing stuck on. Your lips barely move, and nobody hears a thing.

Ayush Kumar

Founder · San Francisco

So far

Five months, in order.

  1. May 2026

    Started.

    Read what had been published on silent speech, then rebuilt the research models on public data before buying any hardware.

  2. August 2026

    Hong Kong, and a rig.

    Flew to Hong Kong for parts and built an eight-electrode rig from hand-modified boards. Accuracy went from 26% to 86% in 17 days.

  3. 24 September 2026

    The demo.

    Six phrases, 90.5% right, typing live into Claude Code, Gmail and WhatsApp. Electrodes taped to my face, no sound, checked by a microphone.

  4. October 2026

    Something you'd wear.

    Moved to San Francisco. Designed a holder that hooks over the ear, printed it, and dropped the tape: 20 command words, 9 in 10 right once it's on.

  5. Now

    The first fifty.

    Other faces are this week's work. The first fifty people decide the final shape with me.

The fastest way to talk to a computer shouldn't need a quiet room.
